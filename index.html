Beautiful, but deliberately schematic.

Shells are quantum states, not containers. Choose a neutral ground-state atom: hydrogen-1 (1 proton, no neutrons), helium-4 (2 + 2), neon-20 (10 + 10), or argon-40 (18 + 22). The isotope fixes the neutron count. Both helium electrons share the 1s orbital with opposite spins. ↑ and ↓ denote spin, not motion.

Reading the cloud: each colour represents one occupied principal shell (n). Dots are possible electron detection locations, not individual electrons. Isolate shells to see their overlapping distributions. Equal samples per electron indicate relative occupancy. These smooth spherical distributions are qualitative teaching models, not calculated orbital densities: radial nodes, individual p-orbital shapes, electron correlation and realistic element-dependent contraction are omitted. Filled p subshells have spherical total density, but this is not an exact many-electron wavefunction. Views are fitted independently; do not compare their displayed sizes as atomic radii.

Optional 90% boundaries: each sphere encloses 90% of its shell’s full illustrative sample set in 3D. These are per-shell sample quantiles, not measured surfaces or joint probabilities for all electrons. Samples extend outside the surfaces. Dashed silhouettes are not electron orbits.

Scale breaks: an atom has a characteristic size around 10⁻¹⁰ m; nuclei and nucleons are on the 10⁻¹⁵ m scale. The nucleus marker is greatly enlarged in the atomic view. Each chapter resets local magnification and crossfades to a new representation. These are discontinuous changes of scale, never a physically continuous zoom. The gluon toggle changes detail within the nucleon view, not physical scale.

Inside a nucleon: proton valence content is uud; neutron is udd. Gluons mediate the strong interaction; a full nucleon also includes gluons and sea quark–antiquark contributions. The ribbons, spheres, sizes and colours are illustrative, not measured shapes, paths or literal colour charges. Quarks and gluons are elementary in the Standard Model; gluons are not components inside quarks. No established quark interior is shown.

Motion & energy: motion is bounded, time-based cinematic choreography, not numerical integration of physical forces. There is no Hamiltonian, physical velocity or calculated energy, so energy-conservation error and numerical energy drift are not applicable. Real isolated physical systems conserve total energy; this animation makes no dynamical prediction.

Controls: click or tap a nucleon in the nucleus to open its matching interior; drag to orbit freely; wheel/pinch or +/− to zoom within a chapter (0.55–2.2×). Select chapters with 1–3 or the bottom buttons. Arrow keys rotate when the scene is focused. Space pauses all cinematic motion; R resets the camera. Reduced-motion preferences start paused. Switch proton/neutron inside a nucleon, and toggle illustrative gluon fields. Hydrogen-1 contains no neutron; its neutron option is comparison only.

Rendering uses a depth-sorted 3D perspective projection on Canvas 2D. Detail adapts to slow frames, pixel density is capped, and hidden tabs stop drawing. The frame counter measures this device, not a universal performance claim.
